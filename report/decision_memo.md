---
title: "SkyLink: medical drone network for Kaveripuram district"
subtitle: "Decision memo for the District Health Officer (M7)"
---

**To:** District Health Officer, Kaveripuram  **From:** SkyLink design team

## 1. Recommendation

**Build a three-hub drone network now, at about ₹2.25 crore a year.**

- **Hubs at three sub-district hospitals: H1, H2 and H4.** Each hub serves the PHCs nearest to it. Every one of the 24 PHCs is reached by drone, flying around the airstrip and the reserve forest.
- **11 drones** (4 at H1, 3 at H2, 4 at H4), **14 spare batteries** and **9 charging points**.
- **One drone at each hub is always kept free for emergencies.** Vaccine, medicine and lab-sample flights never use it.
- **A road vehicle stays on call as the backup.** It takes any emergency that no drone can fly within 30 minutes.
- **Cost: ₹2.25 crore a year**, all in. That covers hubs, drones, batteries, chargers, staff, electricity and the road trips that remain. It is 40 % of our ₹5.57 crore ceiling, which is what sending everything by road would cost.

## 2. What the district gets

| | Today (road) | With SkyLink |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency orders (blood, antivenom) delivered within 45 minutes | almost none (1.5–3 hours) | **89 in every 100** over a year |
| The slowest 1 in 20 emergencies | 3 hours | about 2½ hours (they go by road) |
| All orders flown, not driven | 0 % | **90 %** |
| Annual cost | ₹5.57 crore if every order goes by road | **₹2.25 crore** |

## 3. What we cannot promise: 90 emergencies in 100 within 45 minutes

We set ourselves a target of 90 in 100 (S45 ≥ 0.90). **No network we tested reaches it over a whole year. The weather is the reason, not the number of drones.**

- **Gusts ground every drone in the district for about 1 hour in 20** (477 hours a year). In July it is 1 hour in 4. During those hours the road vehicle is the only option.
- **Strong headwinds** sometimes put the two furthest PHCs (P11 and P17) out of the drone's safe range.
- **Even with unlimited drones, the year tops out at about 89–90 in 100.** Extra money buys almost nothing: the most expensive option we tested costs ₹0.20 crore a year more and gives the same 89 in 100.

## 4. How the plan holds up when things go wrong

**Weather** (each option simulated hour by hour, with thousands of emergency orders):

| Week | Emergencies within 45 min | Orders sent by road |
|---|---|---|
| Calmest week of the year (February) | **97 in 100**; worst day 95 | 4 % |
| A typical year | **89 in 100** | 10 % |
| Worst monsoon week (late July, gusts ground drones 40 % of the time) | **53 in 100**; worst day 3 | 50 % |

**In the monsoon, the road vehicle is part of the system, not a spare. Keep its contract fully staffed from June to September.**

**Busy months and broken drones.** We simulated 500 random months, each with demand between 25 % below and 25 % above today's level, and with 2–10 % of drones out of service on any day.

- Demand and breakdowns barely move the result. Ten percent more orders lowers the score by less than 0.1 point in 100.
- What decides a month is how windy it is.
- The recommended plan hits 90 in 100 in about **3 months in 10** (29 %), and 85 in 100 in about 9 months in 10.

## 5. What would change our advice

| If… | What happens | What we would do |
|---|---|---|
| **Batteries improve by 20 %** (same drone, better cells) | **94 in 100: the 90 target becomes reachable.** Cost falls by about ₹0.11 crore, because fewer orders go by road. | Ask for 180 Wh/kg cells in the purchase specification. This is the single best upgrade. |
| Batteries improve by 20 %, and we buy a lighter, slower drone with the same range | 87 in 100 (worse), about ₹0.09 crore cheaper | Do not trade speed for weight. Keep the same drone and use the better battery for extra range. |
| **Drones become 30 % cheaper** | about ₹0.16 crore a year cheaper; same service | Buy one more drone and more spare batteries for the same money. |
| **Orders grow by 15 % every year** | Service holds (about 89 in 100), because emergencies always fly first. Vaccines and lab samples start going by road, and cost climbs. | Review the fleet every year. Adding drones and batteries step by step keeps about 9 in 10 orders flying, at about ₹2.5 crore in year 1 rising to about ₹4.2 crore in year 5. That stays under the ceiling. |

## 6. Why this option and not another (the decision rule)

We compared three finalists that differ only in drones, batteries and the emergency reserve:

| | Recommended | Best service | Cheapest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual cost | ₹2.25 crore | ₹2.45 crore | ₹2.20 crore |
| Emergencies within 45 min | 89 in 100 | 89 in 100 | 88 in 100 |
| Months that reach 90 in 100 | 3 in 10 | 3 in 10 | 1 in 6 |

**Our rule is the "knee": the point after which more money stops buying better emergency service.**

- The best-service option costs ₹20 lakh a year more for no measurable gain.
- The cheapest option saves ₹5 lakh a year. In return, about 1 more emergency in 100 is late, and a month that reaches 90 in 100 becomes about half as likely.

**We also checked the choice against every scenario above** (calm, typical and monsoon weeks, busy months, battery, price and demand changes). We asked which option leaves the smallest regret in its worst case.

- **The recommended option wins whenever an emergency delivered late is worth more than about ₹5,300 to the district.** That is about twice the ₹2,500 a road trip costs.
- **The cheapest option wins** if a late emergency is valued at only the road-trip cost. It also wins if the fleet were never adjusted for five years while orders double. We do not plan to do that (see section 5).

We think a late bag of blood or antivenom is worth more than ₹5,300, so we recommend the knee.

## 7. Decisions we ask of you

1. **Approve** the three-hub network (H1, H2, H4) with 11 drones, 14 spare batteries and 9 charging points, at about ₹2.25 crore a year.
2. **Keep a road vehicle on contract**, fully staffed in June–September.
3. **Ask suppliers for better battery cells** (about 180 Wh/kg). This is the cheapest route to the 90 in 100 target.
4. **Review the fleet every year** as orders grow, and study a fourth hub near P11/P17 if headwind losses there stay high.

*Numbers come from our own simulation of one year of real hourly wind for the district, and from the district's demand and cost data. Full method: formulation cards M0–M7 and the verification appendix.*
