# M4 — Hub network and fleet: formulation card

**Question.** For each vehicle design (A–E), which sites become hubs, which hub serves each PHC, and how many drones and bays does each hub need, at the lowest annual cost?

**Decision variables (one MILP per design; 8 sites × 24 PHCs):**

- y_j open site j (H0–H6, P09) and x_ij PHC i served by hub j, **binary**
- n_j drones and b_j bays per hub, **integer** ≥ 0
- o_j operators per shift, **integer** ≥ 0 (auxiliary, linearises "1 per 4 drones, ≥ 1 per open hub")
- vehicle design A–E, **categorical**, looped outside the MILP

**Objective:** min annual cost, ₹/yr (costs.yaml, CRF at 10 %)

- hubs ₹6.93 lakh each; drones (₹2 lakh + ₹45k/kg MTOW) × (CRF 3 y + 15 %) plus pack wear; bays ₹23.7k; operators ₹15.1 lakh per post; energy ₹9/kWh grid, ₹28/kWh genset; road ₹2,500 per grounded emergency

**Constraints:**

| # | Constraint | Number |
|----|--------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| C1 | every PHC served (M0 C1) | Σ_j x_ij = 1 |
| C2 | only from an open hub | x_ij ≤ y_j (tight form, no big-M) |
| C3 | reach and 45 min | x_ij = 0 unless L_ij ≤ R and 3 + L/V + 1 + 1 ≤ 45 min (longest 24.9 min, never binds) |
| C4 | drone time (70 % cap) | Σ_i x_ij · sorties_i · (flight + 2 min hover + 10 min ground) ≤ 0.70 × 1440 × 0.945 × n_j |
| C5 | bay-hours per day | ≤ 24·b_j and ≤ (24 − 1.29)·b_j + 2 × 1.29 (only 2 bays in the outage) |
| C6 | staff | 4·o_j ≥ n_j; o_j, n_j, b_j ≥ y_j |
| C7 | linking | n_j, b_j, o_j ≤ N_max·y_j, N_max = need if j served every PHC it reaches (cuts off nothing) |

**Method:** scipy.optimize.milp (HiGHS branch-and-cut): proven optimum in < 1 s per design, no new dependency.

**Result:**

- **D cheapest, ₹1.69 crore/yr:** 4 hubs (H1, H2, H6, P09), 8 drones, 4 bays
- E ₹1.76 crore (3 hubs), C ₹1.83 crore (5 hubs)
- A and B infeasible (6 PHCs / P17 out of reach); what-ifs sending those PHCs by road cost ₹3.73 / 2.21 crore
- all far below the ₹5.57 crore all-road cap

**Verification:** brute force on 4 hubs × 8 PHCs = MILP in 10/10 cases; LP gap 2.4–4.7 % (the LP opens whole hubs, the gap comes from rounding n, b, o); doubling big-M changes nothing; hand check exact.

**Key assumptions and data issues handled:**

- *Reach:* still-air M1 radius including the 15 % reserve; the average-wind range loss (5–9 %) sits inside the reserve, and wind risk is left to M5/M7. Shrinking R for a 6.4 m/s headwind was rejected
- *Averages only:* mean daily demand, no queues, peak hour or 30-min waits (those are M5); the 8 drones are a floor that M5/M6 raise. Peak-hour sizing (17–19 drones) was rejected
- *Sorties:* one order = one out-and-back sortie, no batching; a lab pick-up is the same sortie as a delivery; P09 served from a hub at P09 needs no sortie
- *Wind:* brief headwind model at the mean flyable-hour wind (4.0 m/s, factor 1.05–1.10) for time and energy; direction ignored; one wind record for all hubs; 0.945 = flyable share of hours
- *Energy per sortie:* mean of loaded and empty cruise power × flight time + 60 s hover loaded and empty; charged at 90 %; price weighted by outage hours (3 h × 3/7 = 1.29 h/day)
- *Grounded hours:* emergencies placed in them (6.1 %) wait > 30 min and go by road; routine and lab orders wait and still fly
- *Packs and bays:* one of 500 cycles used per sortie (₹22/Wh × E ÷ 500); spares left to M6; bays get no 70 % cap (the brief caps drone time only)
- *Stock:* every hub holds the items; resupply from the H0 blood bank is not modelled. Demand uses the rescaled hourly profile (M0 data issue 1)
