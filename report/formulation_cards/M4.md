# M4 — Hub network and fleet: formulation card

_At most one page. **AI-drafted — rewrite in your own words before submission.** Status: 🟡 draft._

**Question:** for each M1 vehicle (A–E), which sites become hubs, which hub serves each PHC, and how many drones and bays each hub needs, at the lowest annual cost.

**Decision variables** (one MILP per design; 8 sites × 24 PHCs): y_j ∈ {0,1} open site j (H0–H6, P09); x_ij ∈ {0,1} PHC i served by hub j; n_j, b_j ∈ ℤ≥0 drones and bays (the brief's four); o_j ∈ ℤ≥0 operators per shift (auxiliary, linearises "1 per 4 drones, ≥ 1 per open hub"). The design is categorical, looped outside.

**Objective:** min annual cost ₹/yr (costs.yaml, CRF 10 %): hubs ₹6.93 lakh each; drones (₹2 lakh + ₹45k/kg MTOW) × (CRF 3 y + 15 %) plus battery packs; bays ₹23.7k; operators ₹15.1 lakh/post; energy ₹9/kWh grid, ₹28/kWh genset; road ₹2,500 per grounded emergency.

**Constraints:**
1. Σ_j x_ij = 1: every PHC served (brief's first line; M0's C1).
2. x_ij ≤ y_j (tight form, no big-M).
3. x_ij = 0 unless L_ij ≤ R and 3 + L/V + 1 + 1 ≤ 45 min (never binds; longest 24.9 min).
4. Σ_i x_ij · sorties_i · (flight + 2 min hover + 10 min ground) ≤ 0.70 × 1440 × 0.945 × n_j (brief's 70 % cap; 0.945 = flyable share).
5. Bay-hours/day ≤ 24·b_j and ≤ (24 − 1.29)·b_j + 2 × 1.29 (genset runs 2 bays in the outage).
6. 4·o_j ≥ n_j; o_j, n_j, b_j ≥ y_j. 7. n_j ≤ N_max·y_j (same for b, o); N_max = need if j served every PHC it reaches, so it cuts off nothing.

**In / out:** in: surrogate-FM designs (`m1_fm_designs.csv`), no-fly flight distances, demand, rescaled hourly profile, wind, costs. Out: `ctx["m4_network"]` (hubs, assignments, n, b, o per design) for M5/M6.

**Method:** `scipy.optimize.milp` (HiGHS branch-and-cut): proven optimum in < 1 s per design, no new dependency.

**Result:** **D cheapest, ₹1.69 cr/yr**: 4 hubs (H1 H2 H6 P09), 8 drones, 4 bays. E ₹1.76 cr (3 hubs), C ₹1.83 cr (5 hubs). A and B infeasible (6 PHCs / P17 out of reach); what-ifs sending only those PHCs by road cost ₹3.73 / 2.21 cr. All far below the ₹5.57 cr all-road cap.

**Verification:** brute force on 4 hubs × 8 PHCs = MILP in 10/10 cases; LP gap 2.4–4.7 % (LP opens whole hubs; gap from rounding n, b, o); doubling big-M changes nothing; hand check exact.

**Key assumptions and data issues handled:**
- *Reach:* still-air M1 radius incl. the 15 % reserve ("M1 range with reserve"); average-wind range loss (5–9 %) sits inside the reserve; wind risk left to M5/M7. Shrinking R for a 6.4 m/s headwind was rejected.
- *Averages only* (brief M5): mean daily demand, no queues, peak hour or 30-min waits (M5); the 8 drones are a floor that M5/M6 raise. Peak-hour sizing (17–19 drones) was rejected.
- *Sorties:* one order = one out-and-back sortie, no batching; a lab pick-up is the same sortie as a delivery; P09 served from a hub at P09 needs no sortie.
- *Wind:* brief headwind model at the mean flyable-hour wind (4.0 m/s, factor 1.05–1.10) for time and energy; direction ignored; one wind record for all hubs.
- *Energy/sortie:* mean of loaded and empty cruise power × flight time + 60 s hover loaded and empty; charged at 90 % efficiency; price weighted by outage hours (3 h × 3/7 = 1.29 h/day).
- *Grounded hours:* emergencies placed in them (6.1 %) wait > 30 min and go by road; routine and lab orders wait and still fly.
- *Packs:* one of 500 cycles used per sortie (₹22/Wh × E ÷ 500); spares left to M6. Bays get no 70 % cap (the brief caps drone time only).
- *Stock:* every hub holds the items; resupply from the H0 blood bank is not modelled. Demand uses the rescaled hourly profile (M0 data issue).
