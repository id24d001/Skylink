# M2 — Hub plate topology: formulation card

_Draft written with AI help from the code in `src/m2_plate/`. **Rewrite in your own words before hand-in** (rule 1)._

**Question.** How light can the 160 × 160 × 3 mm 6061-T6 centre plate be while staying within 2.5× the compliance of a solid plate, and what outline do we cut?

**Model and boundary conditions.** In hover the arms push the plate up at the corners and the battery pushes down in the middle, so the plate works in **out-of-plane bending**. I model it as a Kirchhoff plate (ACM element, w and two slopes per node), not plane stress.
- **Supports:** w = 0 on a 10 × 10 mm arm-clamp patch at each corner. Rotations are free (simply supported, conservative: a tube clamp gives a 3 mm plate little rotational restraint). Together the four patches remove all three rigid-body modes. The patches are kept solid.
- **Load:** a uniform pressure, pointing down (−z), over the central 40 × 40 mm. F = 2 g × m_batt, where 2 g is the steepest climb (T_max = 2mg/4) and m_batt = 9.48 kg is the heaviest pack set in the M1 design set (E, 4×B6S-16K). That gives F = 186 N. The load size only scales the deflection and stress; the topology and every compliance ratio are independent of it.
- **Not modelled:** in-plane loads (drag, yaw torque). They are carried by membrane action, which is far stiffer than bending at t = 3 mm.
- **Symmetry:** both mid-lines are symmetry lines, so I model a quarter, with zero slope across the cut and the filter mirrored. This is checked against the whole plate.

**Decision variables.** Element densities x_e ∈ [0, 1], continuous: a 40 × 40 quarter mesh (2 mm, default) or 80 × 80 (1 mm, `SKYLINK_M2_MESH=fine`). The volume fraction vf is swept as a parameter, 0.20–0.50.

**Objective and constraint.** Minimise compliance c = fᵀu subject to K(x̃)u = f and mean(x̃) = vf. SIMP: E(x̃) = E_min + x̃³(E₀ − E_min). x̃ = Hx is the **density filter** (linear hat, r = 6 mm), as the brief asks, not a sensitivity filter. The update is by optimality criteria (move 0.2, stop when the largest change is below 0.01).

**From grey field to a cuttable part.**
1. Threshold at the level that keeps vf.
2. Open with a 6 mm disk to split the shape into members that are ≥ 6 mm and thinner ones. The thin ones are **widened** by 3 mm, not deleted, because deleting them cut the load path at low vf.
3. Close with the same disk to fill slots under 6 mm, then open again so that convex corners get an R 3 mm radius.
4. The result passes the 6 mm test by construction ("every solid point lies in a solid 6 mm disk"), and the narrowest member is also measured.
5. The cut part is re-analysed on a 1 mm mesh. Its outline goes to `results/m2_plate_outline.dxf`.

**Selection.** The lightest cut part with compliance ≤ **2.4×** solid. That is the brief's 2.5× minus a 4 % margin, because the ratio moves about 1 % with the mesh. The sweep runs in 0.05 steps, then 0.01 steps near the limit.

**Result.** vf = 0.32, giving a cut part of vf 0.332 and **108.7 g** (brief formula: 0.332 × 207.4 g + 40 g). Compliance is 2.31× solid (2.33× when designed on a 1.33 mm mesh). The narrowest member is 6.75 mm. Under 186 N, w_max = 0.73 mm (solid: 0.32 mm), and the peak bending stress is 107 MPa against a 276 MPa yield (factor 2.6, already at 2 g). The shape is an X: four tapered arms join a ~65 mm central pad.

**Back into M1 (re-run once).** The plate goes from 0.25 kg to 0.109 kg (−141 g). The continuous optima fall by 229–536 g, which is 1.6–3.8 × the plate saving because mass snowballs. The snapped designs fall by 141–447 g. Parts change for 4 of 5 designs, but every design stays feasible. Both FM0 and the surrogate FM are covered (`results/m2_m1_feedback.csv`).

**Verification** (appendix):
- MBB benchmark: density filter, no filter (checkerboards) and p = 1 (grey).
- ACM element against the Navier solution: 0.03 % error at 64².
- Quarter model against the whole plate: identical.
- Adjoint gradients against finite differences: 1e-6.
- Mesh check.
