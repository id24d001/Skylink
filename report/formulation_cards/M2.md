# M2 — Hub plate topology: formulation card

**Question.** How light can the 160 × 160 × 3 mm 6061-T6 centre plate be while its compliance stays within 2.5× that of the solid plate, and what outline is cut?

**Model:**

- in hover the arms push the plate up at the corners and the battery pushes down in the middle, so the plate works in **out-of-plane bending**: a Kirchhoff plate (ACM element, w and two slopes per node), not plane stress
- quarter model with symmetry on both mid-lines (zero slope across the cut, filter mirrored), checked against the whole plate

**Decision variables:**

- element densities x_e ∈ [0, 1], **continuous**: 40 × 40 quarter mesh (2 mm); 80 × 80 (1 mm) as a check
- volume fraction vf, swept as a parameter, 0.20–0.50

**Objective:** min compliance c = fᵀu

**Constraints:**

| # | Constraint | Number |
|----|--------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| C1 | equilibrium | K(x̃)u = f |
| C2 | material used | mean(x̃) = vf |
| C3 | stiffness of the cut part | compliance ≤ 2.4× solid (brief limit 2.5×) |
| C4 | manufacturable | members ≥ 6 mm, slots < 6 mm filled, convex corners R 3 mm |
| C5 | arm-clamp patches | 10 × 10 mm at each corner, kept solid |

**Method:**

- SIMP: E(x̃) = E_min + x̃³(E₀ − E_min), E_min = 1e-9; x̃ = Hx is the **density filter** (linear hat, r = 6 mm), as the brief asks
- optimality-criteria update, move 0.2, stop when the largest change < 0.01
- *Grey field to cuttable part:* threshold at the level that keeps vf; open with a 6 mm disk, with members thinner than 6 mm **widened** by 3 mm, not deleted (deleting cut the load path at low vf); close with the same disk, then open again for R 3 mm corners. The 6 mm test passes by construction, and the narrowest member is measured
- the cut part is re-analysed on a 1 mm mesh and its outline exported (results/m2_plate_outline.dxf)
- *Selection:* lightest cut part meeting C3, vf in 0.05 steps, then 0.01 steps near the limit

**Result:**

- vf 0.32 → cut part vf 0.332, **108.7 g** (0.332 × 207.4 g + 40 g)
- compliance 2.31× solid (2.33× on a 1.33 mm design mesh); narrowest member 6.75 mm
- under 186 N: w_max 0.73 mm (solid 0.32 mm), peak bending stress 107 MPa vs 276 MPa yield (factor 2.6, already at 2 g)
- shape is an X: four tapered arms join a ~65 mm central pad
- *Back into M1 (re-run once):* plate 0.25 → 0.109 kg (−141 g); continuous optima fall by 229–536 g (1.6–3.8× the plate saving, as mass snowballs); every design stays feasible on both the FM0 and surrogate-FM paths (results/m2_m1_feedback.csv)

**Verification:** MBB benchmark (density filter; no filter → checkerboards; p = 1 → grey); ACM element vs Navier solution, 0.03 % error at 64²; quarter model = whole plate; adjoint gradients vs finite differences, 1e-6; mesh check.

**Key assumptions and data issues handled:**

- *Material:* 6061-T6 handbook values E = 68.9 GPa, ν = 0.33, yield 276 MPa, 2,700 kg/m³ (the brief names only the alloy and density); linear elastic, small deflection, thin plate (t/L ≈ 0.02)
- *Supports:* w = 0 on the corner patches, rotations free (simply supported; conservative, as a tube clamp gives a 3 mm plate little rotational restraint); any 8–15 mm patch is defensible
- *Load:* one case, the battery only, as uniform pressure on the central 40 × 40 mm; F = 2 g × 9.48 kg = 186 N (2 g from T_max = 2mg/4; 9.48 kg the heaviest pack set, design E). The load size scales deflection and stress only, not the topology or the compliance ratio
- *Not modelled:* in-plane loads (drag, yaw torque), carried by membrane action, far stiffer than bending at t = 3 mm; bolt holes (clamps sit on the solid patches, the battery strap goes around the central pad)
- *SIMP settings:* p = 3; filter radius 6 mm = minimum member width (physical, so mesh independent)
- *2.4× target, not 2.5×:* the ratio moves about 1 % with the mesh; the 4 % margin costs 2.4 g (exactly 2.5× would give 106.3 g)
- *Mass:* the brief's formula with the cut part's own area fraction (0.332), not the nominal vf, plus 40 g fasteners
