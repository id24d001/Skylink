# M5 — Discrete-event simulator: formulation card

_At most one page. **AI-drafted — rewrite in your own words before submission.** Status: 🟡 draft._

**Question:** M4 sized the network on daily averages. When orders arrive at random, peak at 10–15 h, wait in queues, and meet real hourly wind, gust groundings and the grid outage, what service level does the M4 network actually give?

**Decision variables:** none are optimised here. M5 is an evaluator, and M6 optimises its inputs. For each configuration the inputs are: vehicle design (categorical, from M1: C, D, E); open hubs and PHC → hub assignment (from M4, fixed); drones n_j, bays b_j and spare packs s_j per hub (integer ≥ 0); and emergency reserve fraction r ∈ [0, 1] (continuous), with k_j = round-half-up(r·n_j) drones held back.

**Outputs (the M6 objectives come from these):** S45 = share of emergency orders delivered within 45 min of the order, by any mode (mean of daily values, with its 95 % CI); P95 emergency delivery time; drone utilisation (dispatch → ready again ÷ 24 h); road-fallback share (all orders, and emergencies only); energy in kWh/day (grid and genset).

**Rules (all from the brief and costs.yaml):** Poisson orders by PHC, class and hour. Ground times: load and take-off 3 min, drop or pick-up 1 min, swap and turnaround 6 min, plus 60 s hover at each end (eq. 5). Flight time and energy use 2LV/(V²−w²), with w = that hour's mean wind. No take-off in an hour with gust ≥ 14 m/s. An emergency still waiting after 30 min goes by road (+120 min). A bay is 1.5 kW at 90 %. Outage Mon/Wed/Fri 14–17: the 3 kW genset runs ≤ 2 bays. Dispatch: emergencies first, and the reserve is held back for them.

**Verification:** one hub, exponential drone time, no wind or outage → mean wait vs Erlang C for 8 (c, ρ) cases. The number of days is set so that every configuration's S45 95 % CI is < ±3 pp. Also checked: a hand-timed sortie, and the reserve, grounding, outage-bay, conservation and reproducibility rules.

**Key assumptions and data issues handled** (the brief does not fix these):
1. **Day:** each run is one 24 h day starting at 00:00 with queues empty, drones idle and packs full. There is no warm-up, and nothing carries over between days.
2. **Days and wind:** wind days are drawn uniformly at random, with replacement, from the 365 days of wind_days.csv. Each day uses its 24 hourly rows. Day 1 is taken as a **Monday**, which sets the outage days (the file gives no weekday).
3. **How many days:** a 30-day pilot gives the day-to-day SD s. Then n = ⌈(t₀.₉₇₅ s / 0.03)²⌉, repeated until the CI half-width on mean daily S45 is < 0.03 for **every** configuration. This gave 73 days.
4. **Random numbers:** day k uses seed 2026 + k for demand. Every configuration sees the same days and orders (common random numbers, reused in M6). The hourly profile is the M0-rescaled one (routine column summed to 1.1993, a planted error).
5. **Orders:** a Poisson count per (PHC, class, hour) at rate = daily mean × hourly fraction, with uniform times inside the hour. One order = one sortie, no batching (as in M4).
6. **Queues:** per hub, FIFO, non-pre-emptive: emergencies first, then routine and lab together. A routine or lab order may not take one of the last k_j idle drones. A queued order that cannot fly now (grounded, or out of range in this hour's wind) is skipped, and the next one is tried.
7. **Grounding:** a sortie may start loading only if both the current hour and the hour of take-off (+3 min) are flyable. Drones already in the air finish their sortie. Dispatch is re-tried on every hour.
8. **Headwind:** w = the hour's mean wind, applied to the whole sortie as in the brief's formula (direction ignored). A sortie takes off only if its energy fits E_use (15 % reserve kept). Otherwise it waits, as when grounded.
9. **Delivery time:** order → dispatch + 3 + leg + 1 hover + 1 drop. A lab sample counts as delivered on landing back at the hub.
10. **Road rule:** only emergencies have a 30 min limit. Routine and lab orders wait. Those still waiting at **24:00 go by road** ("same day"), and their 180 min road time is not used in any KPI.
11. **Batteries:** each drone carries one pack. Default **spares s_j = 0**, as M4 costed. On landing the pack goes to a bay queue (FIFO), and the drone waits for any charged pack, then 6 min swap. A pack is recharged by the energy it used: (Wh/0.9) at 1.5 kW. Packs never degrade within a day.
12. **Outage:** from 14:00 to 17:00, bays above 2 are pre-empted, and their packs resume after 17:00. Energy drawn in the outage counts as genset kWh.
13. **P09:** a PHC that is its own hub (P09 at P09) is served by hand-over at 0 min, with no sortie (as in M4).
14. **Out of scope:** drone breakdowns and availability < 100 % (M7), demand growth (M7), and staff limits on simultaneous dispatches.

**Result:** design D as M4 sized it gives **S45 = 0.851 ± 0.025** (target 0.90). P95 is 150 min, because > 5 % of emergencies go by road. Road share is 45 % (emergencies 14.5 %), and utilisation is only 0.46. The limits are packs and bays, the reserve, and wind, not drone time. One spare pack per drone lifts S45 to 0.882. Even an unlimited fleet over all 365 days reaches only **0.88 (D), 0.88 (C), 0.89 (E)**: 5–6 % of emergencies fall in gust groundings and 5–7 % are out of range in headwind (P11, P17 from H2 sit within 0.1–1.5 km of D's still-air radius). → For M6/M7: fleet alone cannot reach 0.90.
