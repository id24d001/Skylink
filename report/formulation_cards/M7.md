# M7 — Decision and defense: formulation card

_At most one page. **AI-drafted — rewrite in your own words before submission.** Status: 🟡 draft._

**Question:** which of M6's finalists should the DHO buy, and does that choice survive bad weather, busy months, broken drones, better batteries, cheaper drones and growing demand?

**Decision variable:** one categorical choice among M6's top 3 (365-day check), all design E (hubs H1 H2 H4): **knee** (drones 4-3-4, spares 4-4-6, bays 3-2-4, 1 reserve/hub; ₹2.25 cr/yr, S45 0.889), **best S45** (5-4-4, 4-6-5, 3-2-2, 1 reserve; ₹2.45 cr, 0.889), **no reserve** (4-3-4, 5-5-4, 4-2-3; ₹2.20 cr, 0.880), i.e. the picks of the brief's three rules. Under demand growth: the fleet re-tune (integer drones, spares, bays per hub).

**Objectives / measures** (brief M6/M7): annual cost (₹ cr/yr, the M6 cost model), S45 (mean daily share of emergencies within 45 min), P(S45 ≥ 0.90), worst-day S45, road share and P95.

**Decision rule:** the **knee**, i.e. the point after which more money stops buying S45. It is tested by **minimax regret**: regret = cost regret + λ × S45 regret, taken over every scenario, at three prices λ of one S45 point. The DHO set no budget below the ₹5.57 cr cap, and the 0.90 target cannot be reached, so "best service within budget" would pick +₹0.2 cr for +0.03 pp.

**Constraints:** cost ≤ ₹5.57 cr/yr (M0). Every M5 rule is unchanged (costs.yaml). The top-up keeps S45 within 1 pp of year 0 (73 CRN days).

**Method:** wind weeks found in wind_days.csv (20 demand seeds per week day; typical = M6's 365 days and seeds). Monte Carlo N = 500 samples × 30 random days, same draws for all candidates (CRN), Wilson 95 % CI. Sensitivity: M1 re-run at e_b = 180 Wh/kg; re-cost of M6's 3,795-configuration archive and knee re-pick; demand years 0–5 (×1.15/yr) with fleet fixed, then a greedy re-tune.

**Verification** (appendix M7): V1 typical year = M6 365-day numbers exactly; V2 cost model = M6 term by term, M1 re-size at 150 Wh/kg = design E, drone-price hand check, archive re-pick at nominal = M6 knee; V3 Monte Carlo at demand 1, availability 1 = 365-day S45 within CI; V4 Wilson half-width ≤ 5 pp; V5 predicted vs actual (P1–P8, committed before the first run).

**Key assumptions and data issues handled** (the brief does not fix these):
1. Candidates = M6's top 3 on 365 days. All are design E, so the three wind scenarios test the policy, not the network. *User-confirmed.*
2. **Wind scenarios:**
   - "Calm" = the 7 consecutive days with the lowest mean wind (days 40–46).
   - "Worst monsoon week" = the 7-day window ending June–September with the most gust-grounded hours (days 209–215, 67/168 h).
   - "Typical" = all 365 days.
   - The worst case is the worst day's S45, averaged over its demand seeds.
   - *User-confirmed.*
3. **Monte Carlo draws:**
   - Demand ~ U(0.75, 1.25) on every PHC and class; availability ~ U(0.90, 0.98). The brief gives ranges only, so uniform draws are assumed. *User-confirmed.*
   - Each drone is independently out for a whole day with probability 1 − a. A down drone's pack is not used as a spare, and reserve = min(k, drones available).
   - A sample is 30 random days with the draw held fixed, and "S45 ≥ 0.90" means its 30-day mean (one month). The per-day probability is also reported.
4. **Battery +20 %:** the catalogue packs keep their Wh and price per Wh, with mass ÷ 1.2. Two variants are run: (A) the same drone with 20 % more pack energy, and (B) M1 re-sized for the same still-air radius (24 km). Hubs and fleet are unchanged.
5. **Drone price −30 %** applies to drone capex, and therefore to the 15 % maintenance. Batteries, bays and hubs are unchanged.
6. **Demand +15 %/yr:**
   - Hubs are fixed. The top-up re-tunes on cost, because S45 stays flat while road trips grow.
   - Regret uses years 1–2 only, because drones last 3 years (costs.yaml) and are re-bought then. The "all years, fleet frozen" set is also reported.
7. Cost regret uses only whole-year scenarios (a week's cost scaled up to a year is not an annual cost). λ is set three ways:
   - implied by the knee choice (knee vs no reserve: ₹0.055 cr/pp)
   - the road-trip value of 1 pp (₹0.025 cr)
   - high (₹0.20 cr)
8. Out of scope (not M7 items): an extra hub near P11/P17 and a headwind range margin in M1. They are named in the memo as the route to 0.90.

**Result:** the knee holds up: S45 0.97 (calm week), 0.889 (year), 0.53 (worst monsoon week; worst day 0.03, grounding hits all three alike). P(30-day S45 ≥ 0.90) = 0.29 [0.25, 0.33] for the knee, 0.30 best S45, 0.16 no reserve; demand ±25 % and availability barely matter, the wind on the days drawn decides. **Battery +20 % on the same drone lifts S45 to 0.944 (target met) and cuts cost by ₹0.11 cr**; the lighter re-sized drone flies slower (17 vs 20 m/s) and loses 1.9 pp. Drone price −30 % saves ₹0.16 cr; the knee moves to 12 drones, 20 spares. Demand growth: S45 holds, cost grows; re-tuning keeps road share near 10 % for ₹2.5–4.2 cr/yr (years 1–5). The knee is the minimax-regret choice for λ ≥ ₹0.053 cr/pp (≈ ₹5,300 per late emergency); with the fleet frozen for 5 years, no reserve wins. **Decision: the knee.**
